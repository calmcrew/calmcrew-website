The case

Crew rarely leave because they can’t do the job.

They leave because a season asks a great deal of them, and most have never been given any training for that side of the work. Turnover is the part of the cost you can see.

What turnover costs

  • 20–40%

    annual crew turnover on most yachts

  • €15,000

    the minimum cost of replacing one junior crew member, rising in multiples for a head of department

  • $21–30k

    what two yachts that ran their own numbers found it cost to replace a junior crew member

What crew say

  • 1 in 5

    crew reporting stress, anxiety or loneliness on board

  • Half

    have considered leaving the industry

  • 89%

    have never had any mental health training

On land, replacing a technical hire is costed at 100–150% of annual salary, and a director at over 200%.

Turnover and replacement costs: Quay Group, “The true cost of crew turnover”, March 2023. Crew figures: Quay Crew and MHSS crew mental health survey, 2021, 1,019 crew across more than 50 countries.

The load

It’s the load, not the person.

Crew are strong, capable people. The strain comes from the shape of the work.

  • Constant demand

    Long days, guests who expect the best, and being on call even when you’re off.

  • Broken recovery

    Short nights, shared cabins and irregular shifts make it hard to properly switch off.

  • No baseline

    Months away from home, without the people and routines that usually steady you.

Calm Crew gives crew something steady they can take with them.

What the research says

Read the list and you’ve described a season.

In 2022, researchers at King’s College London reviewed sixty-three studies of seafarers’ mental health and wellbeing. The risk factors they found include long working hours, night and irregular shifts, poor sleep, high job demands, long spells at sea and weak team cohesion.

Their conclusion was that maritime managers can take practical steps, including providing education and support.

Calm Crew is that education and support, in a form crew can carry in their pocket.

Brooks, S.K. and Greenberg, N. (2022). Mental health and psychological wellbeing of maritime personnel: a systematic review. BMC Psychology.

The app

A few minutes a day, and a course that builds week by week.

  • Today

    A two-tap check-in on mood and energy, and a Daily practice of a few minutes of slow, easy breathing.

  • Learn

    An eight-week course, opening one week at a time, with short teachings and practices crew can use on duty or in a shared cabin.

  • S.O.S.

    A quiet tab for tough moments, with short practices and routes to a person.

Works without signal. On iPhone and Android. See the app on the home page.

What you see

A clear picture of your crew, and nobody’s private answers.

Management sees a combined picture of how the crew are doing on energy and mood, never anyone’s own answers. Crew choose whether their check-ins count towards it, and the more of the crew who use the app, the clearer the picture becomes.

Captains never see individual answers either. That privacy is built in, because crew need to trust the app before they will use it.

The pilot

A study, not a claim.

The pilot runs with crew on working vessels, for about twelve and a half weeks: four quiet days of check-ins, then the eight-week course, then four weeks of living with it. We will report what we find, including anything that doesn’t change. Until then, Calm Crew makes no claims about results.
